Isolated NTC node for 14S and 7S pack temperature monitoring

Design reference: 100 kΩ NTC sensing, ratiometric 24-bit ADC, floating MCU node per pack, reinforced isolation, RS-485 over 20–40 m to a PC. Part numbers are design picks; confirm pin names, registers and ratings against datasheets before layout.

1. Architecture

One node sits beside each pack. Thermistor leads stay under about 3 m, and only digital data and a 24 V supply run the 20–40 m to the PC. Each node floats, so packs never share a ground through the DAQ.

PACK SIDE (floating)                                  | BUS SIDE (PC-referenced)
NTC x8 -> PTC+TVS -> node N -> Rs/Cf -> ADS124S08      |
          ^ Rref 100k from VEXC            | SPI       |
cal-R + board NTC (self-check)             v           |
                                       STM32G0 --UART--> ISOW7842 --> RS-485 --> Cat6 STP --> USB-RS485 --> PC
                                           |           |  (data + isolated 5 V)      (A/B, +24 V, GND)
                                  photoMOS "OK loop"   |  24 V in -> buck 5 V
Design decision. Digital transport over the long run beats analog: a 100 kΩ source on 40 m of cable next to a charger picks up noise, and a single fault can tie two packs together through the sensor leads.

2. Sensor selection and mounting

  • Part: 100 kΩ, B≈3950, epoxy or glass bead (Semitec 104GT-2 class). Tolerance ±1 % raw gives about ±1 °C; 0.5 % or better parts reduce it.
  • Base is a lagging indicator. During charge the hot spots are the cell core, busbars and terminals. The base reads several °C lower and lags by minutes. Add thermistors on terminals and busbars, between cells where possible, near the BMS, and one for ambient. Plan 4–8 per pack.
  • Fixing: thermal epoxy or pad, a Kapton layer for insulation, and lead wire taped along the surface for 2–3 cm to avoid conduction error. Cover with a foam patch to reduce air influence.
  • Insulation: a thermistor on a pack is separated from pack potential only by its coating. Use coated parts, PTFE or silicone wire rated at least 100 V, and hi-pot at about 500 V DC.
  • Charge window (typical Li-ion, check your cell datasheet): charging is usually permitted about 0–45 °C. A sensible start: warn at 45 °C, alarm at 55 °C, alarm on any channel that rises faster than about 2 °C/min.

3. Analog front end

Ratiometric divider

VEXC feeds Rref (100 kΩ, 0.1 %, ≤25 ppm/°C thin film) into node N, and the NTC returns to AGND. The ADC reference is the same VEXC node, so supply drift and ADC gain error largely cancel.

r = V_N / VEXC = Rntc / (Rntc + Rref)       Rntc = Rref * r / (1 - r)
1/T = 1/T0 + (1/B) ln(R/R0)                  (B model)
1/T = A + B ln R + C (ln R)^3                (Steinhart-Hart)

Per-channel circuit

VEXC --[Rref 100k 0.1%]--+---- Rs 1k ----+--> AINx (PGA on, gain 1)
                         |               |
                         N            Cf 100 nF
 J1.1 --[10 ohm PTC]-----+               |
 (NTC top)               +-- TVS         AGND
                         +-- Cn 100 nF
 J1.2 (NTC return) ------------------------ AGND (one star point)
  • Rs/Cf: Rs limits fault current into the ADC pin; Cf is a charge reservoir and filter. With a 25–77 kΩ source, the pole sits near 30–50 Hz, which suits a signal that moves over minutes.
  • Cn: RF decoupling at the connector, C0G or X7R (film if you want it very clean).
  • TVS leakage: require ≤10 nA at 3.3 V and 85 °C. 10 nA × 77 kΩ is about 0.025 °C, while a 100 nA part would give about 0.25 °C. If no low-leakage array fits, use series R plus two BAS116-class diodes to AVDD and AGND.
  • 10 Ω PTC or fusible resistor per line: limits damage if a lead touches pack potential. It adds a constant 10 Ω (0.01 %) that calibration removes.

5. ADC configuration (ADS124S08 class)

  • Inputs: single-ended, AINx versus AINCOM tied to AGND. Reference: REFP0 to VEXC, REFN0 to AGND (or the AVDD–AVSS option if available).
  • Settings: PGA on at gain 1, 20 SPS with the 50/60 Hz-rejecting filter, chop on for offset drift, internal offset self-calibration at start-up. Scan 10 channels in about 1–2 s.
  • Decoupling: AVDD 10 µF + 100 nF, reference pins 100 nF, DVDD 1 µF + 100 nF, AGND/DGND joined at one point under the ADC.

6. Power tree

24 V bus -> PTC fuse -> SMBJ28A TVS -> reverse-polarity FET -> 5 V buck (TPS62933 / LMR14006)
5 V bus -> ISOW7842 (VDD) -> 5 V isolated -+-> LDO 3.3 V digital (TLV75533) -> MCU
                                           +-> LDO 3.3 V low noise (TPS7A2033) -> AVDD / VEXC
  • Load: about 25–40 mA at 5 V on the isolated side. Check the ISOW load curve.
  • Bus drop: 40 m of Cat6 with paralleled conductors is about 2 Ω per leg. At 60 mA that is about 0.25 V.
  • Heat: ISOW and LDOs dissipate real power; keep them away from Rref and the on-board NTC.
  • Alternative: an isolated 24 V→5 V 1 W module (TRACO TMR 1 or RECOM R1SX class) plus a plain digital isolator, if its isolation rating is high enough.

7. Isolation and RS-485 link

  • Isolator: ISOW7842, or any variant giving two MCU→bus lines (TXD, DE/RE) and one bus→MCU line (RXD). Reinforced 5 kVrms with integrated DC-DC.
  • Why reinforced: a non-isolated charger can put the pack at mains-referenced potential. Treat the pack side as mains-hazardous.
  • PCB: slot under the isolator, no copper in the slot, ≥8 mm creepage and ≥4 mm clearance for mains-derived working voltage (pollution degree 2), separate GND_BUS and GND_ISO planes, optional Y capacitor 100 pF–1 nF between them.
  • Transceiver: slew-limited, ≥±15 kV ESD (THVD1450, THVD1500, SN65HVD72, MAX3485E class). 115200 baud is plenty over 40 m.
  • Bus protection: SM712 TVS on A/B, optional 10 Ω series PTCs, common-mode choke in noisy sites.
  • Topology: daisy chain from the PC. 120 Ω termination at both ends (jumper per node), 680 Ω bias at the master end only.

  • Fault checks: open lead (r near 1), short (r near 0), implausible rate of change, channel 8 outside tolerance, supply monitors.
  • Steinhart–Hart from three points (L = ln R, Y = 1/T in K):
g2 = (Y2-Y1)/(L2-L1);  g3 = (Y3-Y1)/(L3-L1)
C  = (g3-g2)/(L3-L2) / (L1+L2+L3)
B  = g2 - C*(L1^2 + L1*L2 + L2^2)
A  = Y1 - (B + C*L1^2)*L1

PC side: Python with pymodbus, poll at 1 Hz, log to CSV or InfluxDB with Grafana, recompute temperature from the raw ratio with current calibration, and raise a stale-data alarm if a node stops answering (a dead node must never look like a cool battery).

9. Safety and fail-safe behavior

  • OK loop: a 60–100 V photoMOS held closed while healthy; it opens on alarm, sensor fault, poll timeout, or power loss. Wire it in series with a charger or BMS interlock if available.
  • This is monitoring, not protection. Keep the pack's BMS and an independent hardware cutoff (thermal fuse or thermostat in the charge path).
  • Floating node, plastic enclosure. A first fault such as a lead touching the pack is then harmless to the PC. A second fault across two different leads is why each line has the PTC.

10. PCB layout

  • 4 layers: signal, GND, power, signal.
  • Short, symmetric ADC input traces with AGND guard traces.
  • Rref resistors in one row, same orientation and thermal environment, away from heat.
  • Wash flux thoroughly before any conformal coat; surface leakage matters at these impedances.
  • Pluggable 3.5 mm keyed terminals labeled per channel, strain relief, plastic enclosure.

12. Calibration and test plan

  1. Resistor box: substitute 10 k, 33 k, 100 k and 330 kΩ precision resistors on each input; verify r and computed R.
  2. Water bath: seal NTCs in thin-walled tubing or waterproof heat-shrink; put them in a stirred bath with a calibrated reference thermometer at about 0 °C, 25 °C and 55–60 °C; fit per-channel coefficients.
  3. Fault injection: open and short each channel, unplug the bus mid-poll, cut power; confirm the OK loop opens every time.
  4. EMC: run next to the charger at full power. Fix noise with a larger Cn, a ferrite, or cable rerouting.
  5. Soak: 24 h logging of the cal-resistor channel; r should stay within ±0.001.

14. Variants

  • Long analog runs (no node): measure differentially across the NTC (AINP on top, AINN on bottom), same Rref and reference. Use shielded twisted pair per sensor with the shield grounded at the DAQ end only, 1 µF-class filtering, TVS at the connector and a PTC per line. Wire resistance error is negligible, but pickup rises and packs share the DAQ ground.
  • Integrated alternatives: LTC2983 handles thermistor linearization and fault detection on-chip. ADM2587E gives an isolated RS-485 transceiver with integrated power for the bus side only.
  • Wireless: avoid next to chargers unless you accept dropouts; if used, keep the same isolated sensor node and fail-safe logic.
Values are for a B=3950 model and typical parts; verify against vendor tables and datasheets, and against your cell and charger specifications.
