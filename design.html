80-channel NTC temperature monitoring for battery charging

A complete design for measuring the base temperature of battery packs (14S and 7S) while they charge, using about 80 NTC thermistors under the platform, digitised locally and sent over an isolated RS-485 bus to a master microcontroller and then to the host system.

1. Requirements and assumptions

  • About 80 NTC sensors, read by a microcontroller and sent to the host system; op-amps, ADCs and similar parts are used where needed.
  • Sensors sit on the surface the battery packs rest on, to monitor all packs while they charge.
  • Cable run to the host of about 20–40 m, in a noisy charging environment, so galvanic isolation is required.
  • Assumed: 10 kΩ NTCs (B ≈ 3380–3435), 0–80 °C range, ±0.5 °C target, 1–2 Hz update rate (charging temperature changes slowly).
Open input: pack footprint and how many packs sit on the surface at once. This sets the sensor pitch and whether one or two sensors per pack are used (80 sensors = 80 packs at one each, or 40 at two each).

2. System architecture

Running 80 analog pairs over 20–40 m is heavy and noisy. The design digitises close to the sensors and sends only digital data over the long distance: five strip nodes of 16 channels each on one RS-485 bus.

Why not one central board?

A central alternative is possible if sensors are close together: 10 × ADG1608 (8:1) muxes into 10 ADC channels of one STM32, or 5 × ADS124S08 over SPI. With 20–40 m sensor cables it needs shielded pairs per sensor and is more noise-prone, so it is not recommended here.

3. Sensor front end

Each channel is a ratiometric divider: Vout = Vref × Rntc / (Rntc + Rref), with Rref = 10 kΩ 0.1% 25 ppm. The same 3.0 V reference (REF3030) feeds the dividers and the ADC reference, so reference drift cancels. A 1 kΩ + 100 nF filter and an ESD/TVS clamp protect each input.

Computed with the beta model, B = 3435, R25 = 10 kΩ. Beta equation: T = 1 / (1/T0 + ln(R/R0)/B).

  • Self-heating: about 0.27 mW per NTC at 25 °C gives roughly 0.1–0.15 °C. Use a 2.5 V reference, or power the dividers from a GPIO only during scans, to reduce it.
  • Cable effect: with sensors on the strip, leads are only a few millimetres, so cable resistance is irrelevant; the filter handles pickup.

4. Node circuit (16 channels)

The drawing shows 3 of the 16 channels (CH0, CH1, CH15). The mux feeds a unity-gain buffer, which isolates the 10 kΩ sources from the ADC sampling capacitor and removes settling errors after each switch.

Scan: set the mux address, wait 20–50 µs, discard the first sample, then average 8–16 samples. A full 16-channel pass takes under 10 ms.

5. Strip PCB and mechanical placement

Each strip carries 16 SMD NTCs, the node electronics and a daisy-chain connector. Milled slots between sensors reduce sideways heat spread; the pitch p equals the pack width.

The pack rests on copper pucks set in an insulating plate; each puck conducts heat to one NTC.

Thermal design rules

  • Use SMD NTCs (e.g. Murata NCP15XH103, 0603) with a copper pad and thermal vias on the surface side; low thermal mass gives fast response.
  • Make the pad or puck larger than the likely pack misplacement (about 20–30 mm wide), or use two sensors per pack and take the maximum.
  • Use an insulating top layer (1–2 mm FR4 or POM), isolate pucks from each other and cut slots between sensors to limit crosstalk on a metal surface.
  • A base sensor reads lower than the cell core and lags it. Characterise the offset once with a reference thermocouple in a test pack, and alarm on rate of rise (dT/dt) and ΔT over ambient as well as absolute limits.

6. Communication and firmware

Bus: isolated RS-485 (ADM2587E, or ISO1410 with an isolated DC-DC), twisted pair, 120 Ω termination at both ends and bias at the master. Protocol: Modbus RTU (or a framed protocol with CRC16), polled at 1–2 Hz. Proposed node register map:

RegistersContent
0–15Temperature per channel, int16 in 0.01 °C
16Fault bits (open or short per channel)
17–19Firmware version, uptime, error counter
  1. Node: set mux address, settle, discard first sample, average 8–16 samples.
  2. Convert: R = Rref × V / (Vref − V), then beta or Steinhart-Hart, then apply per-channel offset from flash.
  3. Master: poll nodes 1–5, merge into an array of 80 timestamped temperatures with fault flags, and forward over USB-CDC or Ethernet (W5500).

Fault detection: a reading near 0 V indicates a shorted NTC; a reading near Vref indicates an open or broken connection. Example limits are below 0.05 V and above 2.95 V.

7. Power, isolation and safety

  • Nodes run from a 12 or 24 V bus with a local buck to 3.3 V; the REF3030 and analog section have their own decoupling.
  • A 14S pack is around 50–60 V. Isolation at the RS-485 link protects the master and host; keep the strip supply SELV.
  • No NTC lead or exposed copper may be reachable by the pack: use solder mask plus polyimide or the insulating top layer, and check creepage and clearance.
  • If this protects against thermal runaway, add a hardware cutoff (comparator, or node MCU driving a charger-disable relay) that works without the PC. Example starting limits to refine from the cell datasheet: warn 45 °C, trip 60 °C, dT/dt above 2 °C/min.

10. PCB layout checklist

  • Place Rref resistors beside the mux, away from hot NTC pads, so references do not self-heat.
  • Decouple REF3030 with 1 µF and 100 nF; give the mux and op-amp their own 100 nF.
  • Use a solid analog ground pour under the divider and mux area; place the buck and RS-485 at the other end of the board and join grounds at one point.
  • Keep ADC traces short and away from the isolated DC-DC; guard high-impedance nodes.

Commissioning

  1. Check each channel against a reference thermometer at three temperatures.
  2. Measure the pack-to-sensor offset with a thermocouple in a test pack at the real charge current.
  3. Verify open and short detection by disconnecting and shorting NTCs.
  4. Test the bus at the full 20–40 m with termination, under charger noise.
